# Preliminary Findings

## 1. Dataset coverage

The prepared trade dataset contains 257 observations covering 2018–2024.

The analysis includes:
- 7 years
- 9 selected agricultural commodities
- 23 EU partner countries represented in the reported trade observations

After preparation, there are no missing export quantity/value observations and no duplicate partner–commodity–year records.


## 2. Agricultural export value peaked in 2022

Total export value for the selected agricultural commodities followed a non-linear pattern:

| Year | Export quantity | Export value |
|------|----------------:|-------------:|
| 2018 | 810,049.80 | 204,130 |
| 2019 | 585,788.79 | 182,377 |
| 2020 | 369,449.72 | 142,654 |
| 2021 | 434,349.14 | 230,960 |
| 2022 | 523,683.78 | 301,022 |
| 2023 | 756,888.78 | 278,495 |
| 2024 | 463,771.22 | 176,194 |

Export value declined between 2018 and 2020, recovered strongly in 2021, and reached its highest level in 2022.

Between 2018 and 2022, export value increased by approximately 47%, despite export quantity decreasing by approximately 35%.

After the 2022 peak, export value declined in both 2023 and 2024. By 2024, total export value had fallen below its 2018 level.


## 3. Export value and physical export quantity followed different patterns

The year with the highest export quantity was 2018, at approximately 810,050 units, while the highest export value occurred in 2022, when quantity was only approximately 523,684 units.

This divergence suggests that changes in total export value cannot be explained by physical export volumes alone.

The aggregate export-value-to-quantity ratio increased substantially between 2018 and 2022.

However, this should NOT yet be interpreted directly as a change in agricultural prices. The difference may reflect:

- changes in unit export values;
- changes in the commodity composition of exports;
- changes in destination markets;
- or a combination of these factors.

Commodity-level analysis is required before drawing conclusions about prices.


## 4. Exports are highly concentrated by commodity

Linseed and wheat dominate the selected agricultural export basket.

Total export value, 2018–2024:

- Linseed: 832,774
- Wheat: 639,177
- Peas, dry: 11,896
- Lentils, dry: 10,433
- Sunflower-seed oil, crude: 9,650
- Other selected commodities contributed substantially smaller values.

Linseed and wheat together account for approximately 94% of the export value represented in the selected commodity dataset.

Therefore, changes in overall agricultural export value are likely to be strongly influenced by developments in these two commodities.


## 5. Exports are also geographically concentrated

Belgium and Italy were the two largest EU destination markets across the full 2018–2024 period:

- Belgium: 533,176
- Italy: 527,895
- Poland: 208,710
- Germany: 63,464
- Latvia: 54,493

Belgium and Italy together account for roughly two-thirds of total export value in the dataset.

This suggests substantial geographic concentration in Kazakhstan's selected agricultural exports to EU markets.


## 6. The 2022 peak warrants further investigation

The trade-value peak occurred in 2022, coinciding with major disruptions to European and global commodity markets following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

At this stage, the analysis does NOT establish that the invasion, EU sanctions, or related market disruptions caused Kazakhstan's export-value increase.

Instead, the timing provides motivation for further investigation.

The next analysis should determine whether the 2022 increase was associated primarily with:

1. changes in export quantities;
2. changes in commodity-level unit export values;
3. changes in commodity composition;
4. changes in EU destination markets.


## 7. The post-2022 decline is an open research question

Export value declined from 301,022 in 2022 to 278,495 in 2023 and 176,194 in 2024.

The reason for this decline has not yet been established.

The next stage will decompose the change by commodity, destination country, quantity, and unit export value before introducing external explanations.

## 8. Annual growth reveals a sharp 2021–2022 expansion followed by reversal

Year-over-year export-value changes were:

- 2019: -10.66%
- 2020: -21.78%
- 2021: +61.90%
- 2022: +30.34%
- 2023: -7.48%
- 2024: -36.73%

The expansion therefore began in 2021 rather than 2022. Export value reached its maximum in 2022 before declining for two consecutive years.

The particularly large 36.73% decline in 2024 warrants further investigation at the commodity and destination-market level.


## 9. Export value is extremely concentrated in two commodities

Across 2018–2024:

- Linseed accounted for 54.94% of selected export value.
- Wheat accounted for 42.17%.
- All other selected commodities combined accounted for only 2.89%.

Linseed and wheat therefore represented 97.11% of total export value in the selected commodity basket.

This means aggregate Kazakhstan–EU export trends in this dataset are overwhelmingly determined by developments in these two commodities.


## 10. Destination markets are highly concentrated

Belgium and Italy were almost equally important destination markets:

- Belgium: 35.17%
- Italy: 34.83%
- Poland: 13.77%

Belgium and Italy together accounted for 70.00% of export value, while Belgium, Italy and Poland together represented 83.77%.

The selected agricultural trade relationship is therefore geographically concentrated in a small number of EU destination markets.


## 11. The 2018–2024 decline was uneven across commodities

Comparing the first and latest years shows substantial differences between commodities.

The largest absolute declines included:

- Wheat: -25,604
- Peas, dry: -10,437
- Linseed: -976

Meanwhile, several smaller commodity categories increased:

- Sunflower-seed oil, crude: +5,843
- Lentils, dry: +3,718
- Sunflower seed: +399
- Rapeseed/canola oil, crude: +35

Therefore, the lower aggregate export value in 2024 does not represent a uniform decline across all commodities. The change was concentrated particularly in wheat and peas, while several smaller export categories expanded.


## 12. Interpretation of the 2022 peak requires caution

The 2022 export-value peak coincides with major disruption to European and global agricultural markets following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

However, the dataset shows that export growth had already accelerated substantially in 2021 (+61.90%).

The descriptive evidence therefore does not support attributing the entire expansion to events beginning in 2022.

Further analysis should distinguish changes in physical quantities, commodity-level unit values, domestic production, and destination-market composition before external causal explanations are considered.

## 13. Domestic production shows substantial year-to-year volatility

Kazakhstan's agricultural production varied considerably across the 2018–2024 period.

For the two commodities that dominate the selected EU export basket, wheat and linseed, production followed different trajectories.

Wheat production:
- 2018: 13.94 million
- 2021: 11.81 million
- 2022: 16.40 million
- 2023: 12.11 million
- 2024: 18.58 million

Linseed production:
- 2018: 0.93 million
- 2021: 0.78 million
- 2022: 0.85 million
- 2023: 0.36 million
- 2024: 0.76 million

Wheat production therefore reached its highest observed level in 2024, while linseed production remained below its 2018 level.


## 14. Wheat production increased substantially in 2022

Wheat production increased by 38.85% between 2021 and 2022, from approximately 11.81 million to 16.40 million.

This coincided with the year in which the selected agricultural export basket reached its highest total export value.

However, this temporal correspondence alone does not establish that higher wheat production caused the overall export-value peak. Commodity-specific export quantities and values must be compared directly with domestic production.


## 15. Production fell sharply in 2023 before recovering in 2024

Wheat production declined by 26.17% in 2023 before increasing by 53.39% in 2024.

Linseed followed a similarly volatile pattern, with production falling from approximately 845,642 in 2022 to 361,697 in 2023 before recovering to approximately 759,591 in 2024.

The production recovery in 2024 is particularly important because total selected EU export value simultaneously fell by 36.73%.

This divergence suggests that the 2024 decline in EU export value cannot be explained simply by lower domestic agricultural production.


## 16. Wheat production expanded over the full analysis period

Between 2018 and 2024:

- Wheat production increased by approximately 4.63 million.
- Sunflower seed production increased by approximately 0.99 million.
- Lentil production increased by approximately 0.20 million.
- Maize production increased by approximately 0.14 million.
- Pea production increased by approximately 0.03 million.
- Barley production declined by approximately 0.13 million.
- Linseed production declined by approximately 0.17 million.

Wheat recorded the largest absolute production increase among the selected commodities.


## 17. Wheat production growth appears to reflect both cultivated area and yield changes

Wheat harvested area increased from approximately 11.35 million in 2018 to 13.07 million in 2024.

Over the same period, wheat yield increased from 1,228.1 to 1,421.6.

The exceptionally high 2024 wheat production level therefore reflects a combination of a larger harvested area than in 2018 and a comparatively high yield.


## 18. Linseed followed a different structural pattern

Linseed harvested area increased between 2018 and 2021, reaching approximately 1.37 million, despite production declining to approximately 0.78 million in 2021.

This corresponded with a decline in yield from 866.9 in 2018 to 567.7 in 2021.

By 2023, linseed harvested area had fallen substantially to approximately 0.73 million and production to approximately 0.36 million.

In 2024, yield recovered strongly to 869.8 and production recovered to approximately 0.76 million, despite harvested area remaining below its 2018 level.

This indicates that changes in linseed production cannot be understood through cultivated area alone; yield variation was also important.


## 19. Production and EU export trends may be partially decoupled

The preliminary production results reveal an important contrast with the trade dataset.

In 2024, wheat production reached approximately 18.58 million, the highest level observed during 2018–2024, and increased 53.39% relative to 2023.

At the same time, total export value to the observed EU partner markets fell by 36.73% to its lowest level since 2020.

Therefore, domestic production availability alone does not appear sufficient to explain Kazakhstan's EU export performance.

The next stage should directly join production and trade data by commodity and year to examine whether changes in production correspond with changes in EU export quantities and export values.

## 20. EU export intensity differs substantially by commodity

Comparing EU export quantities with Kazakhstan's domestic production reveals substantial differences between commodities.

Linseed was considerably more export-oriented toward the observed EU markets than wheat.

Linseed EU export quantity represented:

- 2018: 32.90% of domestic production
- 2019: 38.07%
- 2020: 20.57%
- 2021: 27.23%
- 2022: 29.28%
- 2023: 26.96%
- 2024: 22.71%

By comparison, wheat EU export quantity represented:

- 2018: 3.18% of domestic production
- 2019: 1.71%
- 2020: 1.04%
- 2021: 1.86%
- 2022: 1.63%
- 2023: 5.42%
- 2024: 1.50%

This indicates that the observed EU market plays a substantially larger role relative to Kazakhstan's linseed production than its wheat production.


## 21. Linseed production and EU exports show partial co-movement

Linseed production and EU export quantity moved in the same direction in several years.

In 2022:
- production increased by 9.04%;
- EU export quantity increased by 17.24%.

In 2023:
- production declined by 57.23%;
- EU export quantity declined by 60.61%.

In 2024:
- production recovered by 110.01%;
- EU export quantity increased by 76.89%.

However, this relationship is not consistent throughout the full period. For example, production increased by 5.06% in 2020 while EU export quantity declined by 43.23%.

The evidence therefore suggests partial rather than systematic co-movement.


## 22. Wheat production and EU export quantities frequently diverged

Wheat shows a substantially weaker correspondence between domestic production and exports to the observed EU markets.

In 2020:
- production increased by 24.51%;
- EU export quantity declined by 24.68%.

In 2021:
- production declined by 17.14%;
- EU export quantity increased by 48.67%.

The most pronounced divergence occurred after 2022.

In 2023:
- wheat production declined by 26.17%;
- EU export quantity increased by 144.70%.

In 2024:
- wheat production increased by 53.39%;
- EU export quantity declined by 57.46%.

These opposing movements demonstrate that domestic wheat production alone does not explain Kazakhstan's wheat exports to the observed EU markets.


## 23. 2023 was an exceptional year for wheat exports to the EU

Despite wheat production falling from approximately 16.40 million in 2022 to 12.11 million in 2023, EU wheat export quantity increased by 144.70%.

The share of wheat production represented by exports to the observed EU markets consequently increased from 1.63% in 2022 to 5.42% in 2023, the highest level observed during the analysis period.

Wheat export value simultaneously increased from 105,421 in 2022 to 220,264 in 2023.

This makes the 2023 wheat trade expansion an important candidate for further contextual investigation.


## 24. The 2024 trade decline occurred despite a strong domestic production recovery

In 2024, wheat production increased by 53.39% and reached approximately 18.58 million, the highest level observed during 2018–2024.

However, EU wheat export quantity declined by 57.46%, while wheat export value fell from 220,264 in 2023 to 64,921 in 2024.

The share of wheat production represented by exports to the observed EU markets fell from 5.42% to 1.50%.

This provides strong descriptive evidence that the 2024 decline in EU exports was not simply the consequence of insufficient domestic wheat production.


## 25. Production availability alone does not explain EU trade outcomes

The combined production and trade analysis shows that domestic production and EU export performance cannot be treated as equivalent processes.

Linseed exhibits some periods of production-export co-movement, particularly during 2022–2024, while wheat frequently shows opposing movements.

The results suggest that other factors — potentially including export prices, destination demand, alternative export markets, logistics, trade conditions, and commodity-market developments — may contribute to changes in Kazakhstan's EU export patterns.

These factors are not directly measured by the present dataset and therefore should be treated as hypotheses or contextual explanations rather than established causal mechanisms.
